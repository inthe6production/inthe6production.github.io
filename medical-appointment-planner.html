How to use it

1. Write each appointment down the moment you book it — date, time, and who it's with — before the slip gets lost.
2. In the days before the visit, fill in the Questions to Ask column while they're fresh in your mind: new symptoms, medication side effects, test results you never heard back about.
3. During the visit, jot what the doctor said in Notes & Next Steps — including the next appointment, before you leave the office.
4. Bring the sheet to every visit, along with your medication list — it's the fastest way to show any doctor exactly what's going on.

Common questions

What size paper does the planner print on?

Standard letter paper (8.5 × 11 inches), turned sideways (landscape). Any home printer can do it — in the print window, choose "Landscape" if it doesn't switch on its own.

What should I write in "Questions to Ask"?

Anything you'd kick yourself for forgetting: a symptom that's new or worse, whether a dose should change, what a test result actually means, and what happens next if you do nothing. Writing it down the day you think of it beats trying to remember it in the exam room.

Eight rows aren't enough — what then?

Print a second sheet and staple them together. Heavy appointment seasons — a new diagnosis, tests, specialists — are exactly when the sheet earns its keep.
