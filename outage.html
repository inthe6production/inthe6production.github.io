Common questions

Can a portable power station run an oxygen concentrator overnight?

For a home concentrator, usually not on its own — the honest math surprises people. A home unit draws about 350 W, so one 8-hour night takes roughly 2,800 Wh of energy, and about 3,300 Wh of power station once conversion losses are counted. That's beyond all but the very largest portable stations. A portable concentrator is a different story: at roughly 90 W it uses about a quarter as much, and a 1,000 Wh station can carry it through a night. Run your own devices through the planner above for your exact numbers.

Why does the heated humidifier change the CPAP number so much?

Heating water takes real power. A CPAP by itself sips about 40 W; turn on the heated humidifier and heated tube and it roughly doubles, to around 90 W. On battery, many people turn the humidifier off for outage nights — one night without it can nearly double how long a station lasts. (If that's uncomfortable for you, size the battery for the humidifier on, like the planner does.)

Why does the planner say a station delivers less than its rated size?

A "1,000 Wh" station doesn't deliver 1,000 Wh to your devices. Converting battery power to household power loses roughly 15%, so about 850 Wh reaches your equipment. Every runtime here is calculated on the delivered figure, not the box figure — that's the difference between a plan and a guess.

What should I do besides buying a battery?

Three free things. First, register with your electricity utility's medical priority or life-support list if they keep one — it can mean priority notice of planned outages. Second, tell your equipment provider you want an outage plan on file; many supply backup bottles or loaner batteries for exactly this. Third, keep a written plan — which devices, in what order, on which battery — somewhere your family or caregiver can find it in the dark.

Generator or power station — which do I need?

Both do different jobs. A power station is silent, safe indoors, and instant — perfect for a CPAP through the night. A generator makes far more energy over a long outage but must run outdoors, away from windows, because of carbon monoxide. For concentrator users facing multi-day outages, the common setup is a station for the night plus a generator for daytime recharging and running — our Generator Wattage Calculator sizes that side of the plan.
