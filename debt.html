Common questions

Avalanche or snowball — which is actually better?

On paper, avalanche (highest APR first) always costs the least interest, because every extra dollar kills the most expensive debt first. Snowball (smallest balance first) usually costs a little more but gives you a fully paid-off debt sooner, and that early win is what keeps some people going. The calculator shows the exact dollar difference on your own debts — it's often smaller than you'd expect, so pick the one you'll actually stick with.

Why is paying only the minimums so slow?

Because a typical minimum is set to barely beat the month's interest plus a sliver of principal. Early on, most of your payment is interest, so the balance barely moves — and the lender collects interest for years. Two ordinary cards paid on minimums alone can drag on for the better part of a decade.

I can only add a little extra. Is it even worth it?

Yes — extra money is aimed entirely at one target debt, so every extra dollar stops accruing interest from that month on, and once that debt is gone its whole payment rolls onto the next one. On typical card debts, an extra $25 a month alone can cut the interest bill by hundreds of dollars and finish months earlier. Try it above: change the extra amount and watch the date move.
