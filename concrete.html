Common questions

How thick should concrete be — driveway vs. shed pad?

Four inches is the standard for both. A shed pad, patio or walkway is fine at 4 inches over a compacted gravel base. A driveway is also 4 inches minimum, but go to 5–6 inches where heavy trucks or RVs park, and use reinforcement (rebar or welded wire mesh) — thickness plus steel is what keeps a driveway from cracking apart.

Bags or ready-mix — when does a truck win?

Under about one cubic yard, bags are realistic: it's a lot of lifting, but one person with a mixer can do it. Past a yard, the bag count, the mixing time and the risk of cold joints (one batch setting before the next lands) make a ready-mix truck the sane choice — the whole slab pours in minutes, evenly. Keep in mind trucks may charge extra for very small loads, so for tiny jobs bags can still win.

Why add a waste percentage?

Because the hole is never exactly the size you measured. Ground dips, forms bow outward, some spills, and some stays in the mixer and wheelbarrow. Running short mid-pour is the worst outcome in concrete — a cold joint is a permanent weak line — so 10% extra is cheap insurance.
