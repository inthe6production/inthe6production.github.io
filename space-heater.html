Common questions

Can you show me an example?

Say the room is 150 square feet with average insulation. At 10 watts per square foot, that's 1,500 watts — about 5,118 BTU per hour — which is exactly the standard full-size plug-in heater. A drafty 200 sq ft room, by the same math, would ask for 2,500 watts: more than any single plug-in heater should give, so the honest answer there is a heater for the corner you sit in, plus fixing the drafts.

Why do all the heaters say 1,500 watts?

Because that's the ceiling for one appliance on a household circuit. Heater shopping isn't really about wattage — it's about the thermostat holding a steady temperature, the safety shut-offs, and how quietly the fan runs. For a smaller room, a 1,500 W heater on its low setting (often 750–900 W) is perfectly fine.

Try our Heating Cost Calculator — enter 1,500 watts and your Ontario electricity rate and it gives you the honest per-month number. Short version: a space heater is cheap for one chilly room and expensive as whole-house heat.

Any safety rules I shouldn't bend?

Three. Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet — never a power bar or extension cord; they overheat. Keep it a metre from curtains, bedding and furniture. And only use a heater with tip-over and overheat shut-off — both models above have them.
