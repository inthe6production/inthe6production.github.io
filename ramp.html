How long does your wheelchair ramp need to be?

A ramp that's too short is too steep to use safely — this is the mistake almost everyone makes. Measure the height of the step, porch or threshold and get the right length at three standard slopes, from the legal standard to an easy, gentle climb.

Measure from the ground straight up to the top of the landing or threshold. A standard house step is about 7–8 inches; two steps plus a threshold is often 14–16 inches; a porch can be 24 inches or more.

Good to know: longer ramps usually need flat landings partway up and at the top, and a permanent ramp often needs a municipal building permit. Portable ramps for a step or two usually don't — but check your local rules before building anything permanent.

Common questions

What is the standard slope for a wheelchair ramp?

The accessibility standard (ADA, and the model most Canadian codes follow) is 1:12 — for every inch of height, the ramp runs 12 inches (1 foot) long. That's the steepest slope considered safe for a permanent ramp. Gentler slopes like 1:16 or 1:20 are much easier for a manual wheelchair user to climb alone, and easier on anyone pushing.

How long a ramp do I need for two steps?

Two standard steps are about 14–16 inches of total rise. At 1:12 that's a 14–16 foot ramp — longer than most people expect, which is exactly why measuring first matters. At the gentler 1:20 slope it would be 23–27 feet. A short portable ramp handles a single low step; two steps usually call for a modular or built ramp with a landing.

Do I need a permit for a wheelchair ramp in Canada?

It depends on your municipality and on whether the ramp is permanent. Portable and threshold ramps generally don't need one; a permanent structure attached to the house often does, and landings, handrails and maximum slope are set by your provincial building code. A quick call to your city building department before building saves expensive rework.
