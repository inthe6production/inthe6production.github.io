Common questions

How deep should mulch be for weed suppression?

Three inches is the standard: enough to block the light weed seeds need to sprout and to hold soil moisture through a dry week. Two inches is a bare minimum that weeds punch through by midsummer. Don't go past about 4 inches — a very deep layer can shed water instead of soaking it in, and piled against tree trunks or stems it traps moisture and invites rot. Keep it a few inches away from trunks and crowns.

Bags or bulk — where's the break-even?

Around 2–3 cubic yards. Below a yard, bags are easy: no driveway pile, no wheelbarrow marathon, and you can do one bed at a time. But a cubic yard is roughly fourteen 2-cu-ft bags, so a 3-yard job is 40 bags to load, haul and open — that's where a bulk delivery dumped in the driveway starts to win, especially if a supplier's delivery fee replaces a second trip.

When should I lay mulch — and what about faded coloured mulch?

Spring, once the soil has warmed and you've weeded and edged the beds — mulch locks in whatever is underneath, weeds included. A thinner top-up in fall helps insulate perennials. And if coloured mulch has faded but is still a good depth, don't replace it: a mulch colourant spray refreshes the colour in place for a fraction of the work (see the slot above).
