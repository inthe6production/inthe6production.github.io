What size air purifier do you need?

Air purifier boxes shout huge room sizes, but the number that actually matters is CADR — how much clean air the machine delivers. The AHAM rule of thumb is simple: your purifier's CADR should be at least two-thirds of the room's square footage (for a standard 8-foot ceiling). Enter your room size and get your minimum CADR.

Common questions

Can you show me an example?

Say your living room is 300 square feet. Two-thirds of 300 is 200 — so you want a purifier with a CADR of at least 200. Of the two above, the WINIX 5500-2 (CADR 232) clears it; the smaller LEVOIT (141) would be working too hard in that room, though it's the right pick for a 200 sq ft bedroom (two-thirds of 200 is 133).

Why does the box claim a much bigger room?

Because that claim usually assumes the machine only has to clean the room's air once an hour. The two-thirds rule cleans it about five times an hour, which is what actually keeps pollen and dust down while you're in the room. When a listing gives both numbers, trust the CADR — it's the tested figure.

What does the smokers-and-pets box do?

It raises the target by half. Smoke and dander are produced continuously, so the guidance for those rooms is a higher air-change rate — for a 300 sq ft room with a smoker, the target becomes a CADR of about 300, which usually means the largest class of home purifier, or two smaller units.

What if my ceilings are higher than 8 feet?

Taller ceilings mean more air to clean. As a rough adjustment, multiply your answer by the ceiling height divided by 8 — a 10-foot ceiling needs about 25% more CADR than the number shown.
