Common questions

Can you show me an example?

Say your basement is 1,200 square feet and feels very damp — musty, cool, damp to the touch. On the guide, 1,200 sq ft rounds up to the 1,500 column, and the "very damp" row reads 35 pints per day. So you'd shop for a machine rated at least 35 pints — in practice, the common 50-pint class gives you comfortable headroom.

What humidity should I aim for?

Between 30% and 50% relative humidity. Below 30% feels dry and can irritate sinuses; above 50% invites mould and dust mites. Most dehumidifiers have a built-in humidistat — set it to 45% and let the machine cycle on its own.

Bucket, hose, or pump — which drainage do I want?

A bucket is fine for a small unit you can check daily. A gravity hose needs a floor drain lower than the machine. A built-in pump is the flexible option: it can push water up into a sink or out a window, which is why pump models suit finished basements without a floor drain.

Will a dehumidifier fix a wet basement?

It manages moisture in the air — it won't stop water coming through the foundation. If you have standing water after rain, deal with the source first (eavestroughs, grading, cracks), then let the dehumidifier keep the air dry.
