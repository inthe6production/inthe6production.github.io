Real life is none of those — owners consistently report 25–40% less range than advertised. Common questions

Why is my scooter's real range so much less than advertised?

Because the advertised number is measured under perfect conditions: a rider around 165 lb, a brand-new battery, flat smooth ground, mild weather, and a constant gentle speed with no stops. Every real-world factor chips away at it — your weight, hills and grass, cold weather, an aging battery, and stop-and-go riding. Industry guides put the real-world figure at 25–40% below the sticker, which matches what this calculator produces under typical settings.

How far should I actually plan to go?

Plan your trips around about 80% of your realistic range — the "plan around" number above. That leaves a reserve so a detour, a headwind, or a longer-than-expected errand never leaves you limping home on a nearly dead battery, which is both miserable and hard on the battery itself.

Does cold weather really cut range that much?

Yes — battery chemistry slows down in the cold. Lead-acid batteries can lose roughly a quarter of their capacity near freezing, and lithium loses some too. If your winter range feels much shorter than your summer range, that's physics, not a broken scooter. Storing and charging the scooter somewhere warm helps.

When should I replace my scooter batteries?

Lead-acid scooter batteries typically last 1–3 years depending on use and charging habits. The classic sign is exactly what this page is about: a trip you used to make easily now drains the gauge. If your realistic range has dropped by a third or more versus when the batteries were new, replacement is usually the fix — and it's far cheaper than a new scooter.

What uses the most range on a trip?

In order: hills and soft ground (grass and gravel can cost 40% more energy), total weight, cold, and speed — full speed drains noticeably faster than three-quarter speed. Smooth, flat, mild-weather routes at a moderate pace are how owners squeeze the most distance out of a charge.
